Know Your Loads.

Every load leaves a signature on your power.
Here is what each one does, and how we fix it.

Which loads run your facility?

Loads

Problem

Seen inTextile mills, automotive plants, pumps, fans and HVAC

Harmonics

Drives draw current in short pulses, so 5th and 7th order harmonic currents flow back into the supply. Transformers and cables run hot, capacitor banks are stressed and breakers trip without an obvious reason.

  • High THDi
  • Transformer heating
  • Nuisance tripping

Seen inSpinning, weaving and processing lines

Harmonics and low power factor

Hundreds of drives and motors run together on ring frames, looms and humidification plants. Harmonics and reactive demand build up across the shift, and kVAh billing rises with them.

  • Rising kVAh billing
  • Capacitor failures
  • Heat in panels

Seen inAutomotive plants and fabrication shops

Fast-changing power factor

Spot welders and presses switch on and off within cycles. Reactive demand swings faster than a contactor panel can follow, so power factor drops and the voltage dips with every weld.

  • Voltage dips
  • Low power factor
  • Flicker

Seen inData centres, telecom and IT campuses

High-frequency harmonics and leading power factor

Server power supplies and UPS rectifiers add harmonics, and at light load they push power factor leading. Triplen harmonics also load the neutral conductor.

  • Neutral current
  • Leading power factor
  • Generator instability

Seen inOffices, hospitals, hotels and schools

Neutral overloading and leading power factor

Many small electronic loads together inject 3rd harmonic currents that add up in the neutral, while LED lighting and IT equipment can push power factor leading.

  • Hot neutrals
  • Leading power factor
  • Unexplained tripping

Seen inSteel plants, foundries and forging

Flicker and harmonic injection

Furnaces draw rapid, unpredictable current. The voltage flickers for everyone on the network and harmonics spread through the plant.

  • Voltage flicker
  • Harmonics
  • Utility complaints

Seen inPumps, conveyors, chillers and air compressors

Lagging power factor

Induction motors draw reactive power to build their magnetic field. With many running, power factor falls and the utility bills for energy the plant never uses.

  • Power factor penalties
  • Higher kVA demand
  • Cable losses

Seen inAutomotive paint lines, electroplating and battery charging

Harmonics across many orders

Large rectifier loads generate significant harmonic content over several orders at once, stressing transformers and any capacitors on the same bus.

  • High THDv
  • Transformer derating
  • Capacitor stress

The Problems These Loads Create.

01

Harmonics

Distortion in the current or voltage waveform caused by nonlinear loads. Degrades equipment, increases heat, and raises energy consumption.

02

Power Factor

The ratio of real power used to apparent power drawn. When low, your facility pays for energy it never actually uses.

03

Voltage Instability

Fluctuations in supply voltage including sags, swells, and transients. Causes equipment malfunction, process interruption, and accelerated wear.
